## Findings

1. **Effort buys luna nothing.** Identical 15/23 at medium/high/xhigh with a *byte-identical* fail set (8 cases). The only thing that scales with effort is cost: 104K → 239K → 303K output tokens. On one verify case the partial score actually *degrades* with thinking (14/15 → 12/15 → 7/15): it thinks its way out of correct attributions.
2. **Rare determinism.** luna-high ran twice the same day, hours apart: same 15/23, same 8-case fail set. AMBER measures stable capability walls here, not sampling luck.
3. **sol never beats luna.** Equal-or-lower at both published bands, plus one extra standing wall (A-24bcf707: sol ✗/✗, luna ✓/✓/✓). No purchasing-relevant difference; skip.
4. **Top-band pathology repeats across families.** W36: astra at xhigh/max regressed (no-code-block, zero-delivery). W37: sol-xhigh hit twin 1800s timeout walls on verify cases and blanked a text paper. luna-xhigh survived the wall but gained nothing. Consistent advice: do not pay for top bands on this backend.
5. **The UI-build case is a family split.** A-d9b79b46: astra passed low/medium/high in W36; luna and sol fail it at every band tested this week. Whatever astra has there, the 5.6 brothers don't.
6. **The shared wall is the frontier.** Eight cases fail for both models at every band (one build, three verify, two review, vision, ui-build). Next case-authoring should study these walls, not the passing middle.

## Verdict

Run **luna at medium**: cheapest published band, identical score to its own top band, same-day replicated. Do not pay for luna high/xhigh — 2.3–2.9× tokens for a byte-identical report card. sol has no band that beats luna at anything this week. For the library: the 8-case shared wall is now the discrimination frontier; the passing middle tells us nothing new.
